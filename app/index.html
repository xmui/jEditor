        Names
        How

        Lay prints on the glass, as many as fit. jEditor previews the glass and puts a box round each print; adjust them, then Scan saves each box as its own photo.

        Load a stack of photos into the feeder, then Scan: the whole batch goes through in one go, each photo showing up as it's scanned. Then load the next batch and press Scan more: the names carry on.

        The scanner's own window opens next: choose the resolution and feeder there. Each photo shows up as soon as it's in.

        Advanced
        Opens the scanner's own window to choose what jEditor can't, like Epson's color restoration or image enhancements. Save there; feeder scans use them from then on.

        A FastFoto starts at brightness 5, contrast −35: its driver sends more contrast than Epson's FastFoto app saves, and this matches the app.

        Click a print to box it, or drag on the glass to draw a box. Drag a box to move it, its corners to resize it, the round handle to tilt it. Delete removes the selected box.

        Camera

        Names

        On the camera: Menu → Network (or Setup) → USB Connection → PC Remote, then plug it into this PC with a USB cable. Photos taken here (F12) or with the camera's own button come straight into the project, RAW files too.

        Let jEditor open your photos

        macOS asks before an app opens files in your folders and on drives. Allow these now and jEditor won't stop to ask while you work.

        jEditor isn't signed by Apple yet, so after an update macOS may ask again. Settings → Projects → Permissions brings this back.
