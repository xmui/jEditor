# Using jEditor

jEditor is a photo viewer for rotating, cropping and straightening folders of photos, built for working through scan orders. It edits the files on your disk directly. Nothing is uploaded.

To install it, see the [README](../README.md). The version number is shown at the top right of Home.

## Using it

1. On Home, click **Open a folder**, or drag a folder onto the window. **Open a folder** opens a card: search your projects and recent folders as you type, drag a folder onto it or click **Browse…**, pick a recent folder, the Photos library (on a Mac), or any project in your projects folder (the Windows, Mac and Linux apps), with a few of its thumbnails. Its foot says where your projects folder is, with a small link to change it. The first time you click **Open a folder** (or drag a folder in), the apps ask once where your projects are kept. There's nothing to choose about saving: edits are kept in jEditor, and **Write edits to file** writes one into its photo (see What it does to your files). Subfolders are included. Home is a table: the last folders you opened lie on it as stacks of their photos, newest first (four at most; three in a small window), each with when you opened it and how many photos it had. A photo you opened on its own in Adjust or Paint lies there too, as one print called **Recent**, while it's the last thing you opened. Point at a stack and it opens upward a little, its photos lifting one after another, showing the photos behind the first (in a theme they're paper prints that fan out); click it to open it again, straight in the grid, or click × to take it off the table (the folder isn't touched). **All projects…** (the Windows, Mac and Linux apps) lists your projects folder, pinned folders and drives. Under the table are the main jobs as big tiles: **Open a folder**, **Scan or Capture** (the Windows app; it's **Import** while a camera's card is plugged in, and **New canvas** where there's no scanner or camera), **Convert**, **Adjust a photo** and **Paint a photo**. At the top right are the version, with a dot on **Settings** when an update is ready, and **Settings**, whose menu has **When jEditor opens** (**Home**, the last project, or a project you pick; a project that can't be reached, such as one on a drive that isn't connected, leaves Home with a message saying so; in a browser, a project that needs asking for permission again waits on the table), Settings, keyboard shortcuts and what's new. It's in Settings → General too. If you open a folder that's inside an order you've opened before, jEditor offers to open the whole order at that folder instead, so the order keeps a single `.jeditor` folder. (For now this only works for orders on the Recent list; jEditor can't yet tell from a folder alone which order it's in.)
2. Move through photos with `←` / `→`. In single view the buttons above the film strip fade away after a few seconds; move the pointer there to bring them back, or turn off **Buttons above the film strip** in Settings to hide them for good. `Space` switches between single view and grid: a photo opened from the grid zooms quickly out of its thumbnail, and shrinks back into it on the way back (not with Animations off, the system's Reduce motion, or a theme). In the grid, the slider at the bottom right or `Ctrl` + mouse wheel makes the tiles bigger or smaller; the highlighted photo stays where it is on screen.
3. Rotate with `[` / `]`. In grid view this rotates every selected photo.
4. Press `C` to crop and straighten. `Enter` saves, `Shift+Enter` saves and opens the next photo, `Esc` cancels.
5. `Ctrl+Z` undoes the last rotation, crop, rename or delete, and `Ctrl+Shift+Z` or `Ctrl+Y` redoes it. The message after an undo has **Redo** too, and the Undo history (`H`) shows what Redo does next above its steps. Every kind of step can be redone: before an undo writes over a photo, its bytes as they are now are kept in `.jeditor/originals`, a move, copy or convert is done again by the same code (asking again about a name that's taken), and a redone delete goes to the bin again, so nothing is lost. Doing something new clears what could be redone.

Changes are saved to the original files as you go.

**Settings** (the gear in the toolbar's **More** section, Home's gear menu, or `Ctrl+,`) has a page for each kind of setting, chosen on the left; it opens on the last one you used.

- **General**: what jEditor opens with (Home, the last project or one you pick); in the Windows, Mac and Linux apps, where your projects and the cache are kept (see [Where projects and the cache are kept](#where-projects-and-the-cache-are-kept)), and on a Mac, Permissions; then **Troubleshooting**: the debug console, the log (the apps), **Clear cache and restart** and **Reset jEditor…**.
- **Appearance**: in the Windows, Mac and Linux apps, a **theme** (see [Themes](#themes)); the theme (dark, light or match the system), the accent color and the color behind photos (black, gray or light, for judging color); on Windows 11 (22H2 or later), **Window background** puts Mica or Acrylic behind jEditor, like the Start menu, and **Transparency** sets how much of the desktop shows through (photos, Crop and Duplicates stay solid; it needs Windows' Transparency effects on, in Settings → Personalization → Colors); then glass (Crystal, the clearest, to start with; Liquid, like Apple's Liquid Glass, which bends what's under its edges; Clear; Opaque, which is faster on older computers; or Lens, a bubble of thick glass; the system's Reduce transparency or Increase contrast makes any of them solid), **Glass strength** (75% to start with: lower shows more of the photo through the glass, higher is easier to read over bright photos; from 75% up the words read over any photo with no shadow behind them, and below that a faint one helps; the icons get a little bolder as the glass gets clearer) and its tint (none, your accent color, warm, cool or rose: none is best for judging color), animations (on, or off: things just change), density and toolbar size.
- **Toolbar & keys**: keyboard shortcuts and their profiles, a vertical toolbar, the buttons above the film strip, which buttons are in the toolbar, and which of Adjust's (Before and after, Reset, Copy, Paste) and Paint's (Undo, Redo) are on their pill rather than under **⋯**. On macOS 26 the Mac's own toolbar is the top row in every mode: in Adjust and Paint it has their buttons, Settings, **⋯** and **Done**, with the photo's name as the window's title, and no second row under it. Right-click the toolbar, or **View → Customize Toolbar…**, to drag buttons in or out with the Mac's own sheet; it changes these same settings.
- **Messages**: which messages pop up after you do something. A tip about something hidden (a key to hold in Paint, rotating outside the frame in Crop) shows once, the first time it helps; **Show tips again** brings them back.
- **Auto crop** (with the Auto crop experiment on): keeping test scans to improve Auto crop, how many are kept, and exporting, recovering or clearing them.
- **Projects**: saving edits (kept in jEditor or written to the file), how a folder opens (watching it for changes, photo libraries, huge folders, the thumbnail cache), stored projects and cleaning up the open one.
- **Updates**: checking for updates, and beta builds.

## Themes

In the Windows, Mac and Linux apps, **Settings → Appearance → Theme** makes jEditor look like an app of another time: the same jEditor, laid out the way that era's apps were, with its own title bar and a menu bar.

- **Windows XP**, in **Royale**, **Royale Noir** and **Royale Red** (the dots on its card): an Office 2003 program, with command bars, a task pane each side and a menu bar.
- **Snow Leopard**, as **Finder** (light) or **Aperture** (dark): one toolbar with the labels under its buttons, a source list and a foot bar.
- **1984**: the first Macintosh, in one bit. Its **Thumbnails** setting shows the grid dithered to one bit (as MacPaint did), in black and white, or in color; the photo you open is always in color.

The menu bar's menus have jEditor's commands, with their keys. The title bar moves the window, a double-click maximizes it, and its buttons minimize, maximize and close it (on a Mac they stand in for the traffic lights, and macOS 26's toolbar steps aside for the theme's). Snow Leopard has no menu bar in the window, as a Mac app never did: on a Mac, the menu bar at the top of the screen has jEditor's commands while a theme is on (File, Edit, View, Photos and Help for Snow Leopard; the theme's own menus for the others). In full screen the title bar goes; the menu bar stays. On Linux the window opens again (just as it was) when a theme goes on or off, so the theme's title bar takes the place of the system's. Adjust and Paint are in the toolbar's ⋯ (or More), the Picture menu, and `E` and `B`. Light or dark is the theme's, and so are the accent color, glass, density and toolbar size: Settings leaves them out while a theme is on. The whole app wears it: the dialogs and Settings, Crop, Adjust and Paint (with their own tool icons), the scan window and every toolbar button. A toolbar with more buttons than fit wraps onto a second row. **jEditor** at the start of the gallery goes back to jEditor's own look.

## Watching the folder

jEditor keeps an eye on the open folder. Photos a scanner saves into the order show up in the grid on their own, in sort order, without moving your place; they glow briefly, and a message offers **Show**, which jumps to them and selects them. Photos deleted, renamed or edited in another program are picked up too. Where the folder can report its own changes, they show up as they happen; on drives that can't report changes, it's checked when you come back to the window and every so often while it's on screen (less often on a slow drive). Turn off **Watch the folder for changes** in Settings to stop this.

`R` rescans by hand. Folders are listed several at a time, which helps most on a network drive, and only new photos are opened, so an unchanged order of 2000 photos rescans in a fraction of a second. Edited photos are then looked for in the background.

## Flags

Mark photos while you work through an order:

- `P` picks a photo and `X` rejects it (a rejected tile dims; a pick takes the reject off), `Shift+X` marks it as needing a rescan, `1`–`5` give it a color label (red, yellow, green, blue, purple; the same key again takes it off) and `U` clears them all. Search finds them with `is:picked` and `is:rejected`. In the grid they apply to the selection, in single view to the photo shown. They're also at the top of the right-click menu, and `Ctrl+Z` undoes them.
- Flags show on the grid tiles, the film strip and beside the file name.
- The **Flags** section at the bottom of the folder list counts each flag. Click one to show only those photos (click again, or the × on the chip in the folder pill, to show everything). `Ctrl+A` then selects just them, ready for **Move to…**, **Export** (under ⋯ on the selection bar) or **Delete**: moving every "needs rescan" photo into a Rescan folder takes two clicks and `M`.
- Right-click a flag in the list to **Copy list of names** or **Save list as text…**: one path per line, for whoever is on the scanner.

Flags are kept with the order, in `.jeditor/flags.json`, so they follow renames and moves made in jEditor and go with the order to another computer.

## Search

`Ctrl+F` or `/` opens Find anything (as `Ctrl+K` does), which finds photos and folders by name as you type, along with commands and projects. Pick one to go to it. The flags in the sidebar's Flags list filter the grid.

## Find anything

`Ctrl+K` (`⌘K` on a Mac), or **Find anything…** in the mode menu (the **Photos ▾** chip, **Adjust ▾** or **Paint ▾** in those modes; Edit in a theme's menu bar), opens one box that finds jEditor's commands, the project's photos and folders, and your other projects as you type. `↑` `↓` choose, `Enter` runs it, `Esc` closes. It works in Photos, Adjust, Paint, Crop and Duplicates, and lists what works there: in Adjust, Auto color and Before and after; in Paint, the tools.

- **Forgiving:** every word must match, in any order. `rot` finds Rotate left and right, `rtl` Rotate left, `delte` Move to trash, `delete` too (other words for the same thing count), and a photo by its name or its folder's.
- **Says what it acts on:** with photos selected in the grid, the commands that act on them say "3 selected".
- **Teaches the keys:** each command shows its key at the right, as you've set them in Keyboard shortcuts.
- **Learns:** what you pick often and lately comes first. Opened with nothing typed, it shows the last few you picked, then what fits: for the selection (Move to…, Star, Rotate, Turn upright, Rename, Export…), or in Adjust and Paint, their usual ones.

## What it does to your files

- **Rotation** (when saved over the original, or without a project). JPEGs are rotated by changing the EXIF orientation tag, so the image data isn't recompressed. This works on scanner files that have no orientation tag yet. PNG and WebP are re-encoded; PNG stays lossless. GIFs aren't rotated, because that would lose the animation.
- **Crop and straighten** (the same). The photo is re-encoded in its original format (JPEG at quality 95). EXIF data (capture date, camera), the ICC color profile, DPI, XMP and IPTC are carried over. The orientation tag is reset, since the pixels are now upright.
- **Rename.** Files are renamed in place. If the new names overlap the old ones (shifting a numbered sequence, say), they go through temporary names first. If a rename fails part-way, every file gets its original name back.
- **Delete.** Photos are moved to `.jeditor/trash` inside the opened folder, not deleted. `Ctrl+Z` restores them.
- **Move to a folder.** Photos are moved on disk, which is a rename and instant even for big scans; where a file can't be moved it's copied and the original removed. A copy is a byte-for-byte duplicate. Cached thumbnails and previews follow the photo, so moving doesn't make them again.
- **Export.** Copies go to a folder named after the project (`Summer 2024 export`; or onto a USB drive, or into a folder you choose), full quality, for the web, for print or as you set them, with any adjustments and other edits.
- **Convert.** Each photo is written again as AVIF, JPEG XL or WebP, upright and in sRGB, with its EXIF (orientation reset, the old thumbnail dropped; the color profile isn't kept, as the pixels are sRGB now). Replacing puts the original in the Recycle Bin or `.jeditor/trash`; `Ctrl+Z` puts it back under its old name.
- **Edits are kept in jEditor** (Lightroom's way), in Photos, Adjust and Paint alike. In a project, crops, straightening, turns, flips, adjustments and painting are kept on the photos instead of being written into them, so a batch of slide scans can be cropped, exported as copies, and cropped again later. A small dot on its thumbnail marks each edited photo (its tooltip: Edited — the original file is untouched, and `Ctrl+S` writes your edits into the file). The dot is amber while the photo is **unsaved**: edited since it was last written into its file or exported. **12 unsaved** counts those photos, at the bottom of the sidebar while it's open, or in a glass pill at the bottom left while it's closed (hidden when there are none); click it for **Save over originals**, **Export…** or **Show these photos** (the grid shows just them, with a chip to take that off). Exporting a photo makes it saved; editing it again makes it unsaved again; `Ctrl+Z` of a write does too. **Info** says when and where a photo was last exported (and if it's been edited since), and the count's menu has **Show exported photos** (or search `is:exported`; `is:unsaved` works too). Exports made before jEditor kept track are found once per project: a copy in the project's export folder (*Smith export*, *Smith – Web*, *jEditor Export*), under the photo's name and newer than the project's last edit, marks that photo exported; with no such copy it stays unsaved. With **Keep edits in jEditor** off, nothing is counted. Nothing nags: the edits are kept with the project (`.jeditor/edits.json`, which also says which edits were exported), and nothing is lost by leaving, closing or switching projects. **Write edits to file** (`Ctrl+S`, in a photo's right-click menu, on a selection, on the grid's right-click for every edited photo, and under **⋯** in Adjust and Paint) writes the edit into the photo file; the original is kept in `.jeditor/originals`, so `Ctrl+Z` puts it back, edit and all. It asks first: **Apply to the originals** does that, and **Save as copies** writes an edited copy next to each photo instead, named as Export names copies (its plain *name* becomes *name edited*), leaving the photo and its edit as they are. **Don't ask again** keeps the answer; **Settings → Projects → Saving → Write edits to file** changes it, or puts **Ask each time** back. **Export** makes edited copies instead and leaves the photos and their edits as they are. Without a project (a photo opened on its own), there's nowhere to keep edits, so they're written into the photo straight away. **Settings → Projects → Saving** has **Keep edits in jEditor** (on to start with): turned off, turns and crops are written into the photo straight away, as without a project (adjustments and painting are still kept, and so is any photo that has an edit kept already). **Clear…** there removes every edit kept for the open project, after asking; the photos on disk stay as they are.
- **Adjust.** Never written into the photo in a project: the settings are kept in `.jeditor/edits.json`, and Export (or **Write edits to file**) writes the result, re-encoded like a crop, with the metadata carried over. Without a project (photos picked on a phone, or dropped in), they're written into the photo when you leave Adjust.

## The .jeditor folder

jEditor keeps its own data in a `.jeditor` folder inside the folder you open:

- `trash/` holds deleted photos.
- `cache/` holds thumbnails and duplicate-finder data.
- `flags.json` holds the flags (stars, rescan flags, color labels).
- `edits.json` holds adjustments (light, color, curves) and edits not saved yet.
- `originals/` holds the photos as they were before a batch Auto crop, so it can be undone.

If a subfolder has a `.jeditor` of its own (because it was once opened on its own), opening the whole order merges it into the order's: its deleted photos and Auto crop originals move across, named after the subfolder, its flags join the order's, and its cache is dropped. Then it's removed, so the order keeps one `.jeditor`.

Because the cache travels with the folder, a second computer opening the same order (on a shared drive, say) gets its thumbnails and duplicate data straight away. The large screen-sized previews aren't stored there, to keep the folder small; they're kept in jEditor's own storage on the PC instead.

Rotating doesn't throw the cache away. A rotation only changes the photo's orientation tag, so the cached thumbnail and preview get the same tag instead of being made again from the full scan. Opening the folder again after rotating a batch is as quick as opening it with no changes.

When an order is finished, use **Clean up folder…** (right-click the grid background, or **Clean up** in Settings). It empties the trash and removes the cache, the flags and the Auto crop originals; your photos aren't touched. Do this before copying an order to a customer: on Windows a folder starting with a dot is not hidden, so `.jeditor` would otherwise go along with the photos, deleted ones included. To stop jEditor writing a cache at all, turn off **Save cache in the folder** in Settings. The trash and the flags are still kept there either way.

Folders opened with earlier versions may have a `.jeditor-trash` folder; Clean up removes that too.

Some programs, mostly older print and lab software, ignore the EXIF orientation tag and will show a rotated JPEG the old way round. Cropping writes the rotation into the pixels, so a photo that has been cropped is always upright.

## Crop and straighten

It works like Lightroom or Apple Photos: the crop frame stays in the middle of the screen and the photo moves behind it.

- Drag a corner or edge to resize. When you let go, the view zooms so the crop fills the screen again. Drag a handle past the edge of the screen to zoom out and make the crop bigger. Hold `Shift` to keep the shape, or `Alt` to resize from the center. On a straightened photo, a side dragged out whose corner meets the photo's slanted edge doesn't stop there: the crop slides out of the way (keeping its size), or when it can't, gets a little shorter, so the side keeps following your pointer to the photo's edge. The opposite side stays where it is.
- Drag inside the frame to move the photo. Drag outside it to rotate.
- Straighten with the slider, or `,` / `.` in 0.1° steps. The photo turns around the middle of the crop and zooms in just enough to hide the corners, so there are never empty corners.
- To level something, hold `Ctrl` (`⌘` on a Mac) and draw a line along a horizon or the edge of a print. The level tool (`L`) does the same without the key.
- **Loupe** (`M`): while you drag a corner or edge, or draw a level line, a close-up of that spot shows in a top corner of the window, at the photo's own pixels, with the crop's edge exactly where it will be saved. It moves to the other corner if your pointer comes near it. It's on to start with; turn it off with the button or `M`.
- **Zoom** for a closer look: scroll on the photo (or pinch on a trackpad) to zoom in and out where the pointer is, or use `+` and `-` and the buttons in the bottom corner. Hold `Space` and drag, or drag with the middle button, to look around. Zoomed in, the view stays put while you drag and straighten; **Fit** (`Z`) goes back to the whole crop, as do a new aspect, a quarter turn, **Auto**, **Previous** and **Reset**. Close-ups use the full-size photo, not the screen-sized copy, once it's loaded.
- **Auto crop is an experiment for now**: turn it on in **Settings → Experimental → Auto crop**. While it's off (as it starts), Auto, Every photo, batch Auto crop (`Shift+A`), the scan window's Auto crop and the Auto crop settings page don't show, and no test scans are kept. Finding the prints on the glass in the scan window stays either way. Turned on, they're all back.
- **Auto** (`Shift+A`) finds the print on a scan, straightens it and crops to its edges. It needs a plain scanner lid or background around the photo (or, when the scan cut the print off at two opposite sides, down the other two), and it leaves a few pixels' margin so none of the bed shows. Turn on **Every photo** to run Auto on each photo as it opens. With `Shift+Enter` (save and next), a scan order then becomes: check the crop, press `Shift+Enter`, repeat.
- **Turn upright** (`Shift+U`, or Turn and flip → Turn upright in the right-click menu) turns the photo, or every photo selected in the grid, the right way up. jEditor's own small network looks at each one (in `upright.js`; how it was trained is in `tools/upright`) and only turns a photo when it's sure, so some are left for you. Each is turned the way the rotate buttons turn them, and one `Ctrl+Z` undoes them all. **Turn upright** in the scan window does it to each photo as it's scanned (on to start with), and turns a photo's back with its front.
- **Batch Auto crop.** In the grid, select photos and press `Shift+A` (or **Auto crop** under ⋯ on the selection bar, or the right-click menu). Auto runs on each of them in the background, with the aspect ratio last chosen in the editor, and saves them. Photos with no print edge, or already cropped to it, are left alone. The originals are kept in `.jeditor/originals`, so one `Ctrl+Z` puts the whole batch back; flip through the results and fix the odd one in the editor.
- `Ctrl+Z` inside the editor undoes the last adjustment; `Ctrl+Shift+Z` redoes it.
- Aspect presets: 4×6, 5×7, 8×10, 11×14, square, 3:4, 16:9, original and free. `X` swaps portrait and landscape. A preset fits inside the current crop, so choosing one after Auto never takes in the bed.
- **Previous** (`P`) applies the last crop again. This is useful when a batch of scans has the same borders.
- **Crop many the same way.** Select photos in the grid, open one in the editor (`C`), crop it and press **Save**: the message that follows offers **Crop the other N the same way**. Or select photos and press `Shift+P` (or **Crop N like the last crop** in the right-click menu) to use the last crop you saved. Each photo gets the same aspect ratio, frame and straighten, relative to its own size: a 4 × 6 stays exactly 4 × 6 on a bigger or smaller scan, and a portrait photo gets the frame turned to fit it, so a landscape crop works on both. Each keeps its own quarter turn. The originals are kept in `.jeditor/originals` (or, in a project, the crops are kept as edits), and one `Ctrl+Z` puts them all back.
- The header shows the output size in pixels, and in inches when the file has a DPI.

## Adjust: light, color and curves

Like Lightroom's Develop, for one photo at a time: press `E` (or the sliders button, or right-click → **Adjust…**). The photo changes as you drag, on the graphics card, so even a big scan keeps up.

Under the histogram, four tools as icons. **Edit** has the sliders, in Lightroom's order: **Basic** (Quick fixes, the look, white balance, light, then vibrance and saturation), then Tone curve, Color mixer, Color grading, Detail and Effects, one open at a time. **Restore** has the scan tools: Restore color, Red-eye and dust. Restore color says what kind of fade it found (a faded print, a red or magenta slide, a red or orange cast, a yellowed print); **Keep the colors** keeps a strong color that's real (a sunset, a field of grass) and fixes only the light and any stain; **Pick gray** sets it from something you click that should be gray. In Photos, a photo's right-click menu has **Restore color**, and the selection bar's ⋯ has **Restore color of** the selection: each on its own, or **as one roll**, for photos faded together (a film, a box of prints), which restores them the same way so a frame with no black or a strong color of its own (a sunset) comes out as the others do. **Heal** has spot removal (Heal and Clone); **Masks** has the masks. Clicks on the photo go to spots or masks only while their tool is shown. To reset one section, right-click its heading (the histogram, for the levels); **Reset all** (`R`) is in the ⋯ menu.

- **Quick fixes**, at the top of Basic: **Auto** sets the levels from the photo, **Restore color** brings back a faded print's color, **Backlight** lifts a photo taken against the light. **Auto color** (`Shift+C`) is in the ⋯ menu.

- **Histogram and levels.** Red, green and blue, with marks at the ends when something clips. The handles under it set the black, mid-gray and white points; the lower two set the output range. Pick **R**, **G** or **B** to set one channel. **Auto** (in Quick fixes) sets the levels from the photo; **Auto color** (in the ⋯ menu) sets each channel on its own too, which takes the color cast off a faded print.
- **Light:** Exposure (in stops), Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks. **Backlight** (in Quick fixes) fixes a photo against a bright sky or window in one click: Shadows up and Highlights down, by how dark and how bright the photo is (`Develop.backlight`).
- **Presence:** **Texture** brings out fine detail (skin, leaves, fabric) or, below 0, smooths it; **Clarity** the contrast of bigger shapes, in the midtones, without halos round edges (below 0, softer); **Dehaze** takes haze and fog off a distant view (below 0, puts it on). They're sized to the photo, so every size of it looks the same; the photo in Adjust shows them a moment after the slider stops.
- **Color:** Temperature, Tint, Vibrance (gentle on colors that are already strong) and Saturation. **Pick gray** (`W`, beside White balance in Basic) sets the white balance from the photo: click something that should be white or gray (a wall, a shirt, the paper's border) and Temperature and Tint take the cast off it. Something very bright or very dark can't tell, so pick a light gray. `Esc` stops picking.
- **Tone curve:** click to add a point and drag it; drag a point off the side, or double-click it, to take it away. The curve never turns back on itself. **RGB** or one channel.
- **Color mixer:** the hue, saturation and luminance of reds, oranges, yellows, greens, aquas, blues, purples and magentas, each on its own.
- **Looks:** film simulations of real stocks, chosen from **Look** in Basic (one line: the look's name, opening a menu of the films by process):
  - **Negative (C-41):** Kodak Portra 160, 400, 800, Ektar 100, Gold 200, UltraMax 400; Fujifilm Superia 400, Pro 400H, C200. Soft contrast and highlights, lifted blacks, color grain.
  - **Slide (E-6):** Fujichrome Velvia 50, 100, Provia 100F; Kodak Ektachrome E100; Kodachrome 64 (K-14). Deep blacks, high contrast and color, the finest grain.
  - **Cinema (ECN-2):** Kodak Vision3 50D, 250D, 500T; CineStill 800T and 50D, whose missing rem-jet backing gives the red **halation** round bright lights.
  - **B&W:** Kodak Tri-X 400, T-Max 100, 400; Ilford HP5 Plus, FP4 Plus, Pan F Plus 50, Delta 3200, SFX 200; Fujifilm Acros 100 II. Each turns color into gray with that film's own sensitivity to red, green and blue (Acros darkens reds and lightens skies; SFX does the opposite), with its contrast and grain.
  - **Creative:** cross process (slide film in negative chemistry), instant, faded print, teal and orange, sepia and selenium toning.

  Choose one to put it on, then set its **Amount** (0–200%); **None** takes it off. A look goes over your own settings, so Auto color and the rest still apply under it. Picking a look opens **Dial in** under its Amount: the look's own Contrast, Fade (lifted blacks), Warmth, a shadow and a highlight tint, Saturation and Vibrance, its Color response (a small mixer of its own: each color's hue, saturation and luminance), and its film (Grain, Grain size, Grain color, Halation, Vignette). Changing one shows the look as **modified**, kept with the photo; **Reset** in the Look menu puts it back as it was made. **Save as a look…** (in the Look menu) keeps the look as you've dialled it in, or with no look on, the photo's own settings, under a name; your looks are under **Your looks** in the menu, and a right-click on one renames, duplicates or deletes it (also under **Change your looks**). A photo done with one of your looks keeps a copy of it, so it stays the same if you change or delete the look, and looks the same on another computer. **Presets** saves your own settings under a name and puts them on again.
- **Color grading:** a wheel each for shadows, midtones and highlights: drag from the middle towards a color to tint them (further out is stronger), and the slider under each makes them lighter or darker. **Blending** widens the three into each other, **Balance** moves where shadows end and highlights begin. Double-click a wheel to clear it.
- **Masks:** for part of the photo. **Linear** (drag across the photo: full where you start, none where you stop, so a sky darkens down to the horizon), **Radial** (drag out from the middle of something: full inside, feathered to nothing at the edge; **Invert** for the outside instead) and **Brush** (paint it on; **Erase** paints it off, with **Size**, **Feather** and **Flow**). **Sky** and **Subject** find them in the photo for you: the sky from the smooth, bright or blue ground along the top (and through gaps in trees), the subject from what stands out from the frame's edges and is sharper than the background. They're worked out from color, brightness and sharpness rather than by an AI model, so they're best on clear cases; paint on them, or **Erase**, to touch them up. Each has its own Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites, Blacks, Temperature, Tint, Vibrance and Saturation; up to eight on a photo. Drag a gradient's handles (or inside it) to change it. **Show mask** tints what it covers red. Masks are kept on the photo as it is on disk, so cropping or straightening later doesn't move them off what they're on.
- **Spot removal:** click a speck of dust, a scratch or a blemish to heal it: the spot is sized to the speck, and its source is picked from nearby where the surroundings match best. Drag from a speck instead to choose where it's copied from. **Heal** blends the source's texture into the color and brightness round the spot; **Clone** copies it exactly. **Red-eye**, in **Restore**: click an eye that's red from a flash and only the red in the circle goes, to the eye's own dark (a brown iris or skin isn't touched); it has no source. Drag a spot to move it, its dashed source to copy from somewhere else, its edge to resize it; right-click it (or `Delete`) to take it off. **Find dust** (in **Restore**) puts a heal spot on every speck it finds on a scan (**Sensitivity**: how faint a speck it looks for; check them, and right-click any that aren't dust), and **Clear found** takes those off again. **Scratches and creases**, in **Restore**: drag along a scratch or a crease and it's taken off, filled from either side of it straight across, so an edge or a horizon it crosses carries on through; the line is fitted to the scratch's width (choose it and **Size** changes it; drag it to move it, right-click to take it off). **Find scratches** puts a line on every scratch and crease it finds (thin, long lines lighter or darker than either side of them; the edge of something isn't one); check them, and **Clear found** takes them off with the dust it found. **Descreen** (in **Restore**, under Printed photos) is for a photo scanned from a magazine, newspaper or book: it finds the printed dot pattern and takes it off, blurring the dots together while keeping the edges; **Descreen** sets how much and **Dot spacing** how far apart the dots are (found for you; set it by hand if it's wrong). It shows best at 100%; it's done on the photo's own pixels, so every size of the photo matches. Spots are kept on the photo as it is on disk, so a crop later doesn't move them; copying adjustments to other photos leaves each photo's spots behind.
- **Detail:** **Sharpening** (how much), **Radius** (how fine: 1 for crisp detail, 2–3 for a stronger look) and **Masking** (higher keeps it to edges, off skin and sky); **Noise reduction** smooths grainy or noisy areas while keeping edges, **Color noise** takes out the colored blotches of a dark or high-ISO scan. These work on the photo's own pixels, so they show at 1:1: the view at the top of the section is the full photo at 1:1, where you click on it (the box on the photo shows where). Export and Write edits to file apply them at full size.
- **Geometry:** **Straighten** turns the photo, as Crop's angle does: drag the **Angle** slider, or click **Draw a line** and drag along something that should be level or upright (a horizon, a door frame). **Perspective** fixes keystoning: **Vertical** below 0 spreads the top out, so buildings shot from below stop leaning back (above 0, the bottom); **Horizontal** does the same for the sides. Its **Auto** finds the photo's straight lines and sets both, so the upright lines stand parallel and the level ones run parallel; Straighten then levels them if they still lean. **Lens** fixes what the lens did: **Distortion** straightens lines that bow out (barrel, wide lenses) or, below 0, bow in (pincushion); **Fringes** takes off the colored edges toward the corners (move it until they go: one way for red and cyan, the other for blue and yellow); **Dark corners** brightens corners the lens darkened, in linear light (below 0 darkens them). The photo keeps its size, so no empty corners show. Perspective and lens are done on the whole photo before its spots and crop (`retouch.js`), lens first. Sync copies the lens fixes, as **Lens fixes**, but each photo keeps its own perspective.
- **Effects:** a **Vignette** (to the left darkens the corners, to the right lightens them) with its **Midpoint**, **Feather** and **Roundness**, film **Grain** with its size and **Grain color** (color film's grain is clouds of dye in each color), and **Halation**, the red glow round bright lights. The vignette is on the photo as cropped.
- Double-click a slider's name to put it back to 0; each section has its own **Reset**.
- **Zoom**, as in Lightroom: `Ctrl`+wheel (`Cmd` on a Mac) or a trackpad pinch zooms about the pointer; `Ctrl+=` and `Ctrl+-` zoom in and out, `Ctrl+0` fits the photo and `Ctrl+1` shows it at 100% (one of the photo's pixels to one of the screen's); double-click goes between Fit and 100% on the spot clicked. Zoomed in, drag the photo (or use the wheel, or two fingers) to move about; hold `Space` and drag to move about with a mask or spot tool out. The chip at the bottom right of the photo shows the zoom, with **Fit** and **100%** to click. Past the screen-sized copy's own pixels, the part on screen is drawn from the full-size photo, so 100% is sharp and shows sharpening and noise reduction as Export makes them, and past 100% the photo's pixels show as sharp squares, as in Photos' viewer (smooth at 100% and below); masks, spots and the before view zoom with it. The code is the Zoom section of `app/adjust.js`.
- `\` shows the photo as it was (before and after): no adjustments and no spots or dust taken out, at any zoom; the crop, straighten and perspective stay, so it lines up with After. `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` undo and redo inside Adjust.
- **Copy** and **Paste** (`Ctrl+Shift+C` / `Ctrl+Shift+V`) carry the adjustments to other photos. In the grid, `Ctrl+Shift+V` pastes onto every selected photo; the right-click menu has it too.
- **Sync** (`Ctrl+Shift+S`, Edit → Sync adjustments…, Sync… in the grid's selection bar once two or more are selected, or right-click a selection), as in Lightroom: select the photos, then click the one to sync from last (it has the outline; right-clicking one syncs from that one). A dialog ticks which groups go: crop (with bulk crop: the same as Crop like the last crop, a portrait frame turned to fit and each photo's own quarter turn kept; unticked when the source has no crop of its own), look, white balance, light, presence, levels, tone curve, color mixer, color grading, detail, lens fixes, effects, masks and spot removal. Masks and spot removal start unticked, since they're placed on one photo's own sky and specks; sky and subject masks are found in each photo, so they never move. A group the source hasn't changed is grayed: ticked, it's taken off the others. Each photo keeps what isn't ticked, and its crop; what's ticked is remembered. One `Ctrl+Z` undoes it on every photo. In Adjust, `Ctrl`+click (or `Shift`+click) photos in the strip to select them as well as the one open (a ring inside marks them); then Sync (`Ctrl+Shift+S`, or right-click the strip) puts the open photo's settings, as they are now, onto them. **Auto Sync** (`Ctrl+Alt+Shift+S`, Edit → Auto Sync, or right-click the strip), as in Lightroom: each setting you change goes onto the selected photos too, a moment after, and only that setting, so each keeps the rest of its own; masks and spots stay where they're drawn. An "Auto sync: N photos" chip at the top says it's on (its ✕ turns it off); it goes off when you leave Adjust or select a single photo, and `Ctrl+Z` in Adjust takes the step off the others too. The code is `app/adjust-sync.js`, its groups `Develop.SYNC` in `develop.js`.
- There's no Done button: what you do is kept as you go. Leave with the mode switch, `Esc` or `Enter` (back to Photos), or click another photo in the strip; `Shift+Enter` goes on to the next photo. `Ctrl+Z` in Photos takes a whole visit's adjustments off again.

Adjustments are non-destructive: the photo on disk isn't changed. They're kept with the project (`.jeditor/edits.json`), shown wherever the photo is, and can be changed again any time, from the original. A small dot on the photo's thumbnail says it has them. An adjusted photo keeps its turns and crops the same way, so everything is applied once, from the original. **Export…** writes copies with the adjustments; **Write edits to file** (`Ctrl+S`) writes them into the photo, keeping the original for `Ctrl+Z`. **Discard edit** takes them off.

## RAW files

RAW files from cameras (DNG, CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF, ORF, RW2, PEF, SRW and the rest) are listed with the other photos and shown from the full-size JPEG the camera puts inside each one, turned as the camera was held. They can be flagged, renamed, moved, cropped, adjusted and painted on like any photo, but a RAW file is never written: its turns, crops and adjustments are always kept as edits, and **Export** (or **Write edits to file**) writes a JPEG copy (`IMG_0042.cr3` → `IMG_0042.jpg`). Editing the raw sensor data itself (rather than its JPEG) comes later.

## HEIC photos

HEIC and HEIF photos (an iPhone's `.heic`, and Sony's `.hif`) are listed and shown like any other, upright, decoded by libheif, a codec that comes with jEditor (a WebAssembly build, so it's the same on Windows, Mac, Linux and the web; not in `standalone.html` opened from disk). Nothing writes HEIC, so a HEIC is treated like a RAW: it can be flagged, renamed, moved, cropped, adjusted and painted on, its turns, crops and adjustments are kept as edits, and **Export** (or **Write edits to file**) writes a JPEG copy with its EXIF (`IMG_0042.heic` → `IMG_0042.jpg`). **Convert** turns HEIC photos into any of its formats, whatever their size. The photo's color profile isn't used yet: an iPhone's Display P3 colors are shown as sRGB, a little duller.

## Photos, Adjust and Paint

jEditor has three modes, like Lightroom's modules: **Photos** (sorting, rotating, cropping), **Adjust** and **Paint**. With the sidebar open, switch along its top; with it closed, the chip at the top left names the mode you're in and its menu switches (it's there in single view, and over Adjust and Paint too). `E` and `B` still open Adjust and Paint, and `Esc` goes back to Photos.

Adjust and Paint have no Done bar: what you do is kept as you go, as in Lightroom (`Ctrl+Z` in Photos takes it off again). In the Windows, Mac and Linux apps, closing the window in Adjust or Paint with something not kept yet asks **Keep your changes?** first: **Keep**, **Don't keep** or **Cancel** (in a browser, it asks the browser's way). Adjust's few buttons (Before, Reset, Copy, Paste) are at the top right. Paint's top is in three places: the file, with Undo and Redo, at the left; the tool's options in the middle; and ⋯ at the right. Its zoom (Fit, 100%) is in a pill at the bottom. A film strip runs along the bottom: click another photo and it opens in the same mode. Their toolbar buttons are in the ⋯ menu.

Drag the inner edge of the sidebar, or of Adjust's or Paint's panel, to make it wider or narrower; jEditor remembers each width, in every theme. Double-click the edge to put it back.

## The sidebar

The sidebar runs down the window's left edge, the full height of it, as Finder's and Preview's do: a quiet tint (on a Mac, the window's own see-through material; on Windows 11, Mica when the window's background is on), small gray headings, compact rows and one soft highlight, on what the grid is showing. `Ctrl+B` folds it away and back; drag its edge to make it wider. Drag the edge in past half the sidebar's narrowest width and it snaps hidden, leaving a thin line at the window's left edge: drag from the line to bring it back out. It comes back as wide as it was, and jEditor remembers that it's hidden. It stays beside the photo when you open one (not on a phone-sized window, where it would cover it). On a Mac the traffic lights sit in its top corner.

From the top:

- **Photos · Adjust · Paint**, the mode switch.
- **The project's name** (the Windows, Mac and Linux apps). Click it for the **project picker**: type to find any project, your recent ones first, the open one ticked; **All projects…** lists every one by name, with your projects folder (click its name to choose another); **New project…** and **Open a folder…** are there too. `↑` `↓` and `Enter` open one, `Esc` closes it. Right-click the name (or its **…**) for **Rename…**, **Show in Explorer** (Finder, Files), **Scan…**, **Export…**, **Split with another window** and **Pin to the sidebar**.
- **The folders** in the project, with how many photos are in each, under **All photos**. The project's own top folder is a row only while it has photos of its own. Right-click the sidebar's empty space for **New folder…**.
- **Recents**: the last four projects you opened (not the open one: it's the heading), with how many photos each has, then **Show all…** (the picker, every project). Right-click one for **Open**, **Open in a new window**, **Rename…**, **Show in Explorer** and **Remove from Recents** (it comes off this list and Home's table; the folder isn't touched).
- **Pinned** folders, and **This PC** (**Locations** on a Mac): each drive with a thin bar of how full it is and how much room is left (`182 GB free`; in red when less than a tenth is left). A drive opens out to its folders; a USB drive has **Eject** when you point at it.

**Rename…** renames a project in place, as Finder does: its name becomes a field, chosen, ready to type over. `Enter` renames it, `Esc` or clicking away leaves it as it was. The folder is renamed on the disk; the open project is closed and opened again under its new name, and its flags, kept edits, thumbnails, pins, the projects folder and Recents all follow it. `Ctrl+Z` (or **Undo** on the message) puts the old name back.

In a theme, the sidebar is the theme's own, as it was: its project card has Scan, Explorer and Export as buttons.

Home's **Adjust a photo** opens one photo on its own in Adjust (in a browser that can't open one file, a folder, in Adjust). Home has three tiles, **Open a folder**, **New project** and **Convert**, and under them a quiet row: **Scan or Capture** (or **Import**, while a camera's card is in), **Adjust a photo** and **Paint a photo**. **New project** asks which kind, in the apps: **A folder for photos** (an empty project in your projects folder, with the name you give it) or **A blank canvas**; the sidebar's and the project picker's **New project…** ask the same. In a browser, where there's no projects folder, it goes straight to the canvas. The canvas and **Paint a photo** ask what to paint on: a **New canvas** (below), or **Open a file**: **An image…** opens a single picture on its own (the painting goes into it when you leave Paint), **A folder…** opens a folder of photos as a project in Paint.

## Paint

Paint over a photo in layers, as a tool like Crop: press `B` (or the brush button, or right-click → **Paint…**).

- **Color:** the brush (foreground) and background squares (click one for the system's color picker), **Swap** (`X`), **Reset** (`D`) and **Choose a color…** (the system's picker, for the brush). Under them, your **saved colors**: click one for the brush, `Alt`-click for the background, **+** saves the brush color, and right-click one to take it off. They're kept between sessions.
- **Tools** in a pill down the left, in ten slots: tools of a kind share one (Select: rectangle, ellipse, lasso, wand; Brush: brush, soft round, hard round, pencil, ink, marker, airbrush, spray, the Painters set and the Retro set; Eraser: eraser, block eraser, soft eraser; Heal: spot heal, heal, clone stamp, and under Tone and Focus, dodge, burn, sponge, blur, sharpen, smudge; Fill: fill, gradient, recolor; Shapes: line, arrow, rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse, polygon), which shows the one you used last, with a small mark in its corner; hold it or right-click it for the others. Their keys pick them too. The tools: **Move** (drag the layer, or what's selected; its corners scale it, its sides stretch it one way, the knob above turns it, `Enter` puts it down). **Free transform** (`Ctrl+T`, or ⋯ → Layer) lifts the layer, or what's selected, the same way (a whole layer's box fits what's on it, not the whole canvas, as it does with Move; a text layer's fits its letters, in any font; what's moved past the canvas's edge isn't cut off: the layers grow to keep it, hidden out there and not written by Export, so it's all there when it's moved back in or the canvas is made bigger), with its **W**, **H** (in % of its size) and **Angle** to type along the top, **Flip ↔** and **Flip ↕**, then **Done** (`Enter`) or **Cancel** (`Esc`). `Ctrl`-drag a corner to distort it, or pick a **Mode** along the top: **Skew** (a side slides along itself), **Distort** (each corner goes anywhere) or **Perspective** (a corner and its neighbor along that side move apart or together, as if tilted away). **Warp** puts a grid of 16 points over it: drag one to bend it smoothly (the corners are its corners; the others pull its edges and middle toward them), drag inside to move it all, **Reset** takes the bend out. Its **Style** bends it to a ready-made shape instead: **Arc**, **Arc lower**, **Arc upper**, **Arch**, **Bulge**, **Shell lower**, **Shell upper**, **Flag**, **Wave**, **Fish**, **Rise**, **Fisheye**, **Inflate**, **Squeeze** or **Twist**, each with a **Bend** (below 0, the other way), **H** and **V** (one side, or the top, bigger than the other) and **↔**/**↕** (bend across, or up and down); drag a point and it's **Custom** from there, to carry on by hand. It works on a layer, what's selected (the selection bends with it) or text; warped text is pixels from then on. A layer that's been transformed keeps its pixels as they were before (⧉ on its row), so transforming it again, even back to its size, comes out as sharp as it was; painting on it, **Make it pixels**, or changing the canvas size makes it plain pixels. A layer that's been turned keeps its angle: transform it again and its box, handles, knob and cursors are turned with it, hugging its edges, **Angle** says how far it's turned, and a transformed layer's **W** and **H** are of its size before its first transform, so 100% puts it back (Undo puts the angle back too), **Rectangle** and **Ellipse select**, **Lasso**, **Magic wand** (by color, with **Tolerance**), **Brush** (Size, Hardness, Opacity, Flow), **Soft round** and **Hard round** (the brush with its edge set), **Pencil** (hard edges), **Ink** (thin when you go fast and thick when slow, and with a pen as hard as you press, starting thin), **Marker** (see-through like a felt pen: it multiplies, so where strokes cross it's darker), **Airbrush** (soft; hold still or go over it again to build it up), **Spray**, the **Painters** brushes, under their own heading in the Brush slot's menu: **Charcoal** (grainy, as on rough paper, the grain staying put on the picture from stroke to stroke), **Watercolor** (see-through, pooled darker at its edges, and darker again where strokes cross), the **Texture brush** (paints through a **Texture** chosen along the top: Canvas, Paper or Sand; go over it again to fill it in) and the **Mixer** (starts with the brush color and blends in the colors it goes over, carrying them along the stroke; **Wet** is how much it picks up), the **Retro** brushes, listed under their own heading in the Brush slot's menu (inspired by MS Paint and Mario Paint; every picture in them is drawn for jEditor): the **Pixel pencil** (square pixels on a grid the brush's size, crisp at any zoom), the **Spray can** (hard dots, more the longer you hold it) and the **Stamp** (a little picture with each click, or a row of them as you drag: a star, heart, house, note, sun, leaf, cat or bolt, in the foreground color), **Your brush** (under **Yours** in the Brush slot: one you made, its **Tip** chosen along the top), **Eraser** (`E`; `E` again for the **Block eraser**, a hard square, then the **Soft eraser**, which takes off more as it's held), **Spot heal** (`J`: paint over a mark, a speck or a scratch, and as you let go it's filled from a place nearby that matches what's round it, in the colors round it), **Heal** (`J` again: `Alt`-click where to copy from, then paint over what to hide; what's copied takes on the colors and brightness round the stroke, the way Adjust's spot removal heals) and the **Clone stamp** (`S`: the same, copied as it is). A + shows where they copy from; with **Aligned** on, each stroke goes on from the same distance away, and off, each starts again from where you `Alt`-clicked. With **All layers** they copy the picture as it looks (this layer and those under it) onto this layer, so they can heal onto an empty layer above the photo. In the same slot, under **Tone**: **Dodge** (lighter) and **Burn** (darker), mostly in the **Shadows**, **Midtones** or **Highlights**, and the **Sponge** (less color, or more); under **Focus**: **Blur**, **Sharpen** and **Smudge** (the colors under the brush dragged along). Each has a **Strength**; going over a place again in a new stroke adds more. Like heal, with **All layers** they work on the picture as it looks, onto this layer, **Fill** (by color, with a **Tolerance**, looking at the photo and all the layers or just this one; **Close gaps** (Small, Medium, Large) fills a line drawing whose lines have small gaps as one area, up to its lines, instead of leaking out through them; or **Whole layer**, which fills all of the layer, or all that's selected), **Gradient** (from the foreground color to the background color, or to nothing), **Recolor** (click a color in the picture and it becomes the foreground color everywhere, or everywhere in what's selected, keeping its shading: a red shirt's highlights and shadows turn into the new color's; **Range** is how far from the color clicked it reaches, and **All layers** recolors what you see, onto this layer, rather than this layer's own paint), **Line** (with an arrow if you like), **Arrow**, **Rectangle**, **Rounded rectangle** (its **Corners** along the top), **Ellipse**, **Polygon** (click each corner; double-click, `Enter` or a click on the first corner closes it, `Esc` lets it go) (their **Style**: **Outline** in the brush color, **Filled** with it, or **Outline and fill** with the background color inside, as in MS Paint; each goes on a layer of its own, a shape layer, unless **New layer** in its options is off: with that layer chosen and its tool out, changing the line, fill, opacity or colors changes it, moving or transforming it keeps it a shape, and painting on it or **Make it pixels** in its right-click menu makes it pixels), **Text** (click, type, `Ctrl+Enter`: it goes on a layer of its own, marked T, which keeps the words, so clicking it with the Text tool, or double-clicking the layer, opens it to change the words, font, size or color; **Font** opens a list of fonts, each shown in its own typeface: jEditor's five, then every font on the computer (in a browser, only Chrome and Edge can list them, after asking), with a search box and the ones used last at the top; a picture whose font isn't on this computer shows its text in Sans, the font's name grayed, until it's there; **Align** lines its lines up **Left**, **Center** or **Right**, and **Outline** puts a line that wide round the letters in the background color; moving or transforming it keeps it text, painting on it makes it pixels, as does **Make it pixels** in its right-click menu), the **Color picker** and the **Hand**. Each tool's options are along the top, in the middle; in a narrow window what doesn't fit (the least used first) is behind **›** at their end.
- **Brushes:** with the brush, pencil, spray or eraser, the wheel over the photo sizes it (`[` and `]` too; on a Mac's trackpad, `Alt` and two fingers), and while it changes the brush shows on the photo as it'll paint, with its size in pixels (in the middle of the photo when you use the Size slider). `Shift`-click paints a straight line on from where the last stroke ended. `Alt` + right-drag sizes the brush (sideways) and sets its hardness (up for harder), with the brush shown as it'll paint. Hold `Alt` (on a Mac, `⌥`) with a brush, the fill, a shape, the gradient or text and it's the color picker: click or drag, with a loupe up and to the right of the pointer showing the pixels under it big, and its ring the color picked (above) and the color you have (below). A pen's pressure makes the brush smaller as you press lighter; **⋯ → Pen pressure** makes it the brush's opacity instead (lighter as you press lighter), or both. A pen's **tilt** lays the round brushes and the Painters brushes on their side, longer the further it leans, as a pencil or a stick of charcoal is. **Brush presets:** **⋯ → Brushes → Save the brush as a preset…** keeps the brush with its settings (its size, hardness, texture and the rest, and how a pen works it, but not the color) under a name; pick it in **⋯ → Brushes** to have that brush back as it was, or delete it there. **⋯ → Smoothing** (Some, More or Most) steadies a shaky hand: the stroke follows the pointer a little behind, as if on a string, and catches up where you let go. **⋯ → Symmetry** paints each stroke again, mirrored through the middle of the picture (**Left and right**, **Top and bottom** or **Both ways**) or turned round it (**Round**, 3, 4, 6 or 8 times), with its lines shown faintly while a brush is chosen; heal and clone paint once. **Your own brushes:** select part of the picture and pick **⋯ → Brushes → Make a brush from what's selected…** (or **Make a brush from a picture…**), and name it. What's dark in it paints (in a picture with see-through parts, its shape does), cropped to that and up to 256 pixels across; Size is its long side, and it paints in the brush color. It's kept between sessions, listed in **⋯ → Brushes** (pick one there, or delete one) and as **Your brush** in the Brush slot. **QuickShape:** at the end of a stroke with the brush, pencil or eraser, hold still for a moment and it becomes the shape it looks like: a straight **line**, an **arc**, a **circle** or **ellipse**, a **rectangle** or **square** (its name shows by the pointer). Still holding, drag to turn and size it (a line or an arc from where it started, a closed shape round its middle); let go to keep it. A scribble that isn't a shape stays as drawn. It's on to start with; **⋯ → QuickShape** turns it off. Sizes are the photo's own pixels, kept the same part of the photo from one photo to the next: a brush that's a tenth of the way across a big photo is a tenth of the way across a small one, and the Size slider gives most of its length to the small sizes a small photo needs.
- **The ⋯ menu** on the bar holds **Image** (**Crop…** (`C`), **Canvas size…**, Rotate right, left or 180°, Flip left to right or upside down: the photo's own turn and flip, kept as its edit as Photos' are, never written into the original, with the layers going with it; the turns in a project), **Select** (select all, nothing, invert, feather, grow, shrink) and **Layer** (new, duplicate, merge down, bring in an image, delete), each with its key.
- **Crop and canvas size:** **Crop** (`C`) puts a frame on the canvas. Drag its edges or corners in to crop, or out past the picture to make the canvas bigger on that side. Drag inside it to move it, or outside it to draw a new one, then `Enter` (or Crop) crops and `Esc` leaves it as it was. **Canvas size…** takes a width and height in pixels and adds room (or takes it off) evenly on all sides, and **Canvas back to the photo** undoes both. What's added is the background color, and you can paint on it. The layers reach as far as the canvas does, so what's painted there is kept, and Export, Write edits to file and the viewer show the bigger canvas. Each is one step of Undo, and turning the photo turns the canvas with it. The canvas is up to three times the photo's size. Photos' own Crop and Adjust still show just the photo's part.
- **Masks:** **⋯ → Layer → Mask** (or right-click a layer) adds a mask to the layer: **Add a mask** (all of it shows), **Add a mask from the selection** (only what's selected shows) or **Add a mask that hides it all**. Its thumbnail shows beside the layer's, white where the layer shows and black where it's hidden. With the mask chosen (click its thumbnail; a ring shows which you're painting on), painting black hides the layer there and white shows it again (the colors become black and white; `X` swaps them), and the eraser hides. The layer's own pixels are never changed, so you can bring back what you hid. Click the layer's thumbnail to paint on the layer again. **Invert the mask** swaps what shows and what's hidden, **Apply the mask** puts it into the layer's pixels, and **Delete the mask** takes it off. Masks are kept with the layers, and Export and the viewer show the layer through its mask.
- **Selections** keep painting inside them. `Shift` adds to a selection, `Alt` takes away; `Ctrl+A` selects everything, `Ctrl+D` nothing, `Ctrl+Shift+I` inverts it; **Feather…**, **Grow…** and **Shrink…** (in the ⋯ menu's **Select**) soften its edge or move it out or in by so many pixels, round at the corners; `Delete` clears what's selected; `Ctrl+C` / `Ctrl+V` copy and paste it. With nothing selected, `Ctrl+C` (or **Copy the layer** in a layer's right-click menu) copies the whole layer, and `Ctrl+V` makes it again as a layer above, keeping its mask, opacity, blend, text, shape or filter settings: in the same place in the same picture, or in another picture with the two photos' middles lined up. It goes on the system clipboard too, as it looks, to paste into another app. **Copy merged** (`Ctrl+Shift+C`) copies the photo and every layer shown, as they look together, and **paste in place** (`Ctrl+Shift+V`) puts what you copied back where it came from (`Ctrl+V` puts it in the middle of the view). A screenshot or picture copied in another app comes in on a layer of its own.
- **Colors:** the foreground and background swatches (click to choose), a palette (`Alt`-click for the background color), **Swap** (`X`) and black and white (`D`). `Alt`-click with a brush picks a color from the photo.
- **Layers:** add, duplicate, move up and down, merge down, delete, hide, rename (double-click), each with its **Opacity** and a **Blend** mode (Multiply, Screen, Overlay and the rest). Right-click a layer for its menu. **Lock transparency** (`/`, a lock on the row) keeps painting, fills, shapes and text to what's already on the layer, each pixel as see-through as it was, so you can recolor a shape without going over its edge; the eraser and `Delete` leave a locked layer alone. **Lock the layer** (a filled lock on the row) keeps everything off it: no painting, moving or transforming until it's unlocked. **The photo** is the locked row at the bottom: painting goes on the layers above, so it's never changed. To paint on the photo itself, click its lock (or right-click it → **Unlock the photo**): a copy of it, as it's shown with its adjustments, becomes an ordinary layer named **Photo**, to paint on, erase, move, mask or delete. The photo itself isn't drawn under the layers after that, so what's erased from that layer is see-through (or the canvas's color), in what you see, Export and Save over original. Adjust doesn't change the copy, and the original file is never touched; Undo puts the photo back. **Clip to the layer below** (`Ctrl+Alt+G`, marked ↳) shows a layer only where the layer under it has paint, as through a stencil, and not at all while that layer's hidden. **⋯ → Layer → Merge visible** (`Ctrl+Shift+E`) puts the layers shown into one, as they look together, leaving the hidden ones; **Flatten** does the same and drops the hidden ones. **Adjustment layers** (⋯ → Layer → **New adjustment layer**: **Brightness/Contrast**, **Hue/Saturation**, **Levels**, **Black & white**, **Invert**) change everything under them without touching it, so they can be changed or taken off later: a card by the layers has its settings (double-click the layer, or right-click → **Its settings…**, to open it again), and **Done** keeps them as one step. Painting on one paints its mask: black keeps it off there, white puts it back. Its opacity sets how strongly it applies. **Filters** (⋯ → **Filter**: **Gaussian blur**, **Motion blur**, **Unsharp mask**, **Add noise**, **Median**, **High pass**) go on as layers too, as adjustment layers do: each changes everything under it, its settings stay in its card to change later, and its mask keeps it off where it's painted black. Their sizes are in the picture's own pixels, so they look the same at any zoom and in what's exported. **Groups:** `Ctrl+G` (or right-click → **Group it**, or **Group with the layer below**) puts a layer in a group, shown as a row of its own above its layers, which are set in under it. A layer made while one in a group is chosen joins it. The group's eye hides all its layers, ▾ closes it in the list, and right-clicking its row renames, merges (its layers into one, as they look), ungroups or deletes it. Moving a layer (the arrows under the list) past a group's edge first takes it out of the group, or puts it in; `Ctrl+Shift+G` takes it out and puts it just above. `Alt`-click a layer's eye to show it alone (again for all of them), and `Ctrl`-click its thumbnail (`⌘`-click on a Mac) to select what's painted on it (`Shift` adds, `Alt` takes away).
- **Images:** **Bring in an image…** under the layers (`Ctrl+Shift+O`), drop an image file on Paint (or drag a photo onto it from the film strip), or `Ctrl+V` an image copied in another app. It comes in on a layer of its own, named after it, fitted to the picture and left floating: drag it to move it, a corner to size it (it keeps its shape while the lock between **W** and **H** is closed, as it is to start with; `Shift` frees it, or click the lock to open it), a side to stretch it, the knob or just outside a corner to turn it, and `Enter` puts it down. The pointer shows what a drag does where it is: resize arrows on the corners and sides, turned as the box is, a turning arrow outside the corners, and the move arrows inside. RAW files come in from the JPEG inside them. (`Ctrl+V` pastes Paint's own copy instead when that's the newest thing you copied.)
- **A blank canvas:** right-click a folder (or the grid's background) → **New blank image…**, or the start page's Paint. Pick a size from the list (prints at 300 dpi in inches, screens and social posts, the photo you're looking at, the last one you made) or type one in pixels, inches or centimeters at a resolution (72 to 600 dpi), up to 10,000 pixels a side, either way round; a background (white, black, none or a color); and the folder to save into (with no project open, you choose one and it opens as the project). It's saved as a PNG carrying its resolution, so it prints at that size, and opens in Paint straight away.
- The wheel zooms (with a brush, `Ctrl`+wheel or a pinch); hold `Space` (or the Hand) and drag to move about; `0` fits the photo, `1` is 100%. **⋯ → View** has these too, and turns the view (not the picture) for drawing at a comfortable angle: **Rotate the view left** and **right** (`<` and `>`, 15° a time), or `Shift`-drag with the Hand to turn it freely, or hold `R` and drag (the tool you had comes back when you let go; `R` tapped on its own is still the rectangle, and tapped twice puts the view straight). Its angle shows in the zoom pill at the bottom; click it (or **Put the view straight**) to straighten it. **Snap** (`Ctrl+;`, on to start with) puts a selection's or shape's corners, and the edges or middle of what you're moving, onto the canvas's edges and middle, and the edges and middles of what's on the other layers shown, when they come within a few pixels on screen, with a pink line to show it; hold `Ctrl` (on a Mac, `⌘`) while you drag to move it without snapping. What's moved (not turned or sized) goes in whole pixels, so it stays crisp and can go a pixel at a time. `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` undo and redo inside Paint. On a touch screen, tap with two fingers to undo and three to redo. **⋯ → History** shows the steps under the layers, each by name (Brush, Fill, New layer, Brush: Circle…): click one to go back to it (the later ones are grayed, and come back if you click them), or drag along the list to scrub back and forth. Each `Ctrl+Z` (and redo) puffs what it takes away or brings back: just that part fades, softens and lifts for a moment. It's off with **Settings → Animations**, with reduced motion and in a theme, and going along the history doesn't puff. Painting something new after going back drops the grayed steps.

Painting goes on last, over the photo as it's cropped and adjusted, so it looks as you painted it. It's non-destructive like the rest: the layers are kept with the project (in `.jeditor/paint`), so the painting can be opened and changed again, and a crop later leaves it where it was. **Export…** writes it into copies, and **Write edits to file** (`Ctrl+S`, or **⋯**) into the photo, flattened; the original is kept for `Ctrl+Z`. Without a project (photos picked on a phone, or an image opened on its own), leaving Paint paints into the photo.

## Drives, cards and discs

**Export…** under the **…** by the project's name at the top of the sidebar (or right-click it, or **Export…** under ⋯ on the selection bar; in a theme, **Export** on the project's card) opens one window for taking photos off this computer. Or right-click a USB drive or card in the sidebar for **Export [project] to [drive]…** (copies of the photos) or **Copy [project] to [drive]…** (the whole folder as it is), or a disc drive for **Burn [project] to a disc…**. Each choice says what goes and whether it fits before anything starts, and drives and discs show up as they're put in:

- **USB drive or card** (Windows, Mac and Linux): the whole project, copied in one step, with how far it's got. Each drive is listed with the room left on it. The photos are copied as they are on disk, with no `.jeditor` folder: the flags and edits stay on this computer. Tick **Take its flags and edits too** (off to start with) to put them in a `.jeditor` folder in the copy, so when it's opened in jEditor on another computer they're all there. **Eject when done** ejects the drive after.
- **CD or DVD** (Windows): the burner, whether there's a blank disc in it and whether the project fits, the disc's name, and (with edited photos) whether to save over their originals first, since the disc gets the photos as they are on disk. **Eject when done** brings the disc out after.
- **Copies** (everywhere): an export window like the ones in other photo editors. The settings are in sections that stay closed until you need them, and each closed section says what it's set to, so the whole export can be read at a glance. **Photos**: the selected ones, all of them, the starred, or the edited ones. **Export location**: **Export to** the project, a USB drive or a folder you choose; the **Subfolder**'s name (`Summer 2024 export`); and **Existing files**: to start with you're asked when a name is already there, as when copying files in Windows: **Keep both** (the copy numbered: `Brick wall (2).jpg`), **Replace**, or **Cancel**, which stops the export there; with more photos to come, **Do this for all** answers for the rest. Or set it to always choose a new name, overwrite, or skip, without asking. **File naming**: the name in pieces, typed or added with the buttons: `{name}` the photo's own name, `{project}`, `{date}` (the file's, `2026-11-14`) and `{n}` (its number, `001`), with an example from the photos being exported that changes as you type. **File settings**: the **Image format** (the original, JPEG, PNG, TIFF, WebP, AVIF or JPEG XL), its **Quality** (60–100%) where it has one, and the **Color space** (the photo's own profile, or sRGB for screens). **Image sizing**: full size, or **Resize to fit** a long edge (4000, 3000, 2048, 1600, 1080 px or any other); a smaller photo is never enlarged. **Metadata**: all of it, all except the location, or none. **After export**: show the folder in Explorer (Finder, Files) in the apps, and **Eject when done** for a USB drive. No presets come with it: **Save as preset…** keeps the settings as they stand under a name of your own, shown as a pill to pick again (its name goes in the subfolder's: `Summer 2024 – Client proofs`); change a setting and it's no longer picked; **Remove preset** takes the picked one away. **Show preview** puts the first photo beside the sections, with the name and size it will have; it's off to start with and remembered. The line by the buttons says how many photos, about how big, and where they're going, and the button says how many it exports (**Export 12 photos**). Edits go in the copies, and RAWs come out as JPEGs. In the project, the folder is left out of it to start with, so Rescan doesn't list the copies as the project's photos: the Export to menu has **Leave them out of the project** to change that.

In the Windows, Mac and Linux apps:

- **Importing from a camera's card:** when a card with a DCIM folder is plugged in, jEditor offers to import it; or press `Ctrl+I` (in a browser, choose the card's folder). The photos go into the open project, a new project in your projects folder (named for the day and the card), or a folder you choose; into a folder per day if you like; only the ones not imported before; and the card is ejected after if you like. RAW files come too; videos stay on the card.

## The Photos library (Mac)

The Mac app has your Photos library in the sidebar, under Locations (`photosLibrary`). Open it out for Favorites, Recently added and your albums; click one to open it as the project, or **Open** for the whole library. macOS asks once whether jEditor may see it (and it's in jEditor → Permissions…).

- **Editing a photo from it** works as for any photo: rotate, flip, crop and straighten, Adjust, Paint, Write edits to file. jEditor starts from the photo **as it is now**, with whatever was done to it before in Photos or another app, and the edit is saved back into Photos on top of that, as a Photos edit. So it shows in Photos and on your other devices, the earlier edits are kept in it, and Photos keeps the untouched original: **Revert to Original** in Photos goes back to it. `⌘Z` in jEditor puts the photo back as it was before (saved as one more edit, so nothing in Photos is lost). macOS may ask you to allow jEditor to change each photo.
- Photos sees jEditor's edit as finished pixels: Photos' own sliders for the earlier edits can't be moved again after it (Revert to Original still undoes the lot). jEditor keeps its own edits re-editable until they're saved, as it does for any photo.
- **Renaming, moving and deleting** library photos are left to Photos, so those aren't in the menus. Flags and kept edits are kept on this Mac, for each album.
- **Add to Photos library** (`photosAdd`), on a photo's right-click, under ⋯ on the selection bar, or **Add all to Photos library** on the grid's right-click, puts a folder project's photos into Photos as they are on disk, with their dates, DPI and the rest of their metadata, in an album named after the project (made the first time). A scan order becomes an album. If some have edits kept in jEditor, it asks you to save over their originals first, so Photos gets the photo as it looks here.

## Converting photos: format, quality and storage

**Convert…** turns photos, one or many, into another format: **AVIF** (smallest), **JPEG XL** (newest), **WebP** (for the web), **JPEG** (opens anywhere), **PNG** (lossless) or **TIFF** (lossless, for print). Open it from the toolbar's **Convert** button (under ⋯ to start with; Settings can put it on the toolbar; in single view it converts the photo open), **Convert** on Home, right-clicking a photo, the selected photos, a folder or the project in the sidebar, or ⋯ on the selection bar.

- **Photos:** what's being converted: the project, a folder in it or the selected photos. **Choose a folder…** picks any folder on your computer instead, with the photos in its folders too, without opening it as the project (from the start page, it asks straight away). A folder chosen keeps its originals in the Recycle Bin, or in a `.jeditor/trash` of its own, and **Put the originals back** at the end takes the conversion back.

- **Quality** goes from smaller to better, with a mark at the lowest quality that still looks the same as the original (worked out on one of your photos; *Closest to the original* where only the highest keeps its grain). PNG and TIFF keep every pixel, so they have no quality to choose. Under it, a close-up of that photo at its own pixels, the original beside the result, with their sizes.
- **Storage:** what the photos take **Now** and **After** and by folder, worked out from a sample of them before anything happens, and again as you change the format or quality: what's freed striped green, or (PNG and TIFF, which often take more than a JPEG) how much more striped amber.
- **The originals:** **Replace them** (the converted photo takes the original's place and the original goes to the Recycle Bin, or jEditor's trash; one `Ctrl+Z` puts them all back) or **Keep both** (the converted copies go into `jEditor Converted` in the project, which jEditor doesn't list as the project's photos).

Flags and edits go with each photo, and so do its date taken, camera and the rest of its EXIF (into a TIFF: its camera and dates). RAW files, GIFs and photos already in that format are left as they are, and so, in the lossy formats, are photos that wouldn't get smaller (but not HEIC photos, which are converted so other apps can open them).

While it works, 100 dots stand for the photos, each in their own colors once they're done; done dots turn hollow and green for the storage freed, as many as the share freed so far. Pause or Stop, or close the window and carry on: the corner says how far it's got. At the end: the storage freed, and the size before and after.

AVIF and JPEG XL are written by codecs that come with jEditor (WebAssembly builds of libavif and libjxl), and TIFF by jEditor itself (8 bits, LZW, lossless, which every program that opens TIFF reads; all the photo's EXIF goes in, GPS too). jEditor shows JPEG XL and TIFF photos with its own decoders, where the browser engine can't: TIFFs uncompressed or LZW, Deflate or PackBits, 1 to 16 bits, color, gray, CMYK or a palette (not JPEG-compressed ones yet). Edits on an AVIF, JPEG XL or TIFF photo are always kept as edits (as on a RAW); **Write edits to file** writes them in the same format. Not in `standalone.html` opened from disk, which can't load the codecs.

## Folders (collating)

Some orders are collated: the photos go into labeled folders. Open the order folder and switch to the grid (`Space`).

- **The folder list** on the left shows every folder, including folders inside folders and empty ones, with how many photos are directly in each. Click a folder to see just its photos. `Ctrl+B` hides the list; the grid then shows a folder's subfolders as tiles at the start, and the folder pill shows where you are. `Backspace` goes up a folder.
- **All photos** (top of the list, or the switch in the folder pill at the top left) shows every photo in the order, whatever folder it's in, with each photo's folder named on its tile. It's what you see when an order is opened. `A` switches between All photos and the last folder.
- **Moving.** Drag the selected photos onto a folder, in the list, a folder tile or the breadcrumb. Hold `Ctrl` (or `Alt`) when you let go to copy them instead. Or press `M` (`Shift+M` copies), or use **Move to…** on the selection bar or the right-click menu. That opens a list of folders: type to find one, or type a new name to make that folder and move the photos into it in one go. `Wedding/Speeches` makes a folder inside another.
- **Name clashes.** If a photo with the same name is already in the folder, you're asked what to do: keep both (the moved photo gets a number, `IMG_0042 (2).jpg`), skip it, or replace the one that's there, which goes to the trash. You can apply the answer to the rest of the clashes. Cancel and nothing is moved.
- **Folders.** `Shift+N`, right-clicking the grid or the sidebar's empty space (**New folder…**), or in a theme **New folder** at the bottom of the folder list, makes one inside the folder you're looking at. Right-click a folder to rename it, make a folder inside it, make a **New blank image…** in it (to paint on), or delete it once it's empty.
- In a folder, `←` `→`, the film strip, `Ctrl+A` and **Rename all…** cover that folder's photos only.
- **A photo's information: title, caption, keywords and the rest.** Info (`I`) shows them on top when the photo has them. **Edit**, at the top of Info, turns it into a short form: title, caption, keywords (type one and press `Enter`; × takes one off), rating (click a star; the same again for none), date and time taken, author (several separated by `;`), copyright and place (latitude and longitude; paste a pair into either box). **Done** (`Enter`; `Ctrl+Enter` in the caption) saves, **Cancel** (`Esc`) doesn't, and going to another photo or closing Info saves what you typed. It's saved straight into the photo, as XMP (and the date and place in its EXIF too, where Windows and phones look), without encoding the picture again: JPEG, PNG, WebP and TIFF. A RAW, HEIC, AVIF or JPEG XL gets an `.xmp` file beside it (*scan.xmp* for *scan.cr2*), as Lightroom does. Where the photo already has a field in Windows' own tags or an older app's IPTC, that's changed too, so no app shows the old value. Not kept in jEditor like a crop: only the information changes, so nothing can be lost. `Ctrl+Z` puts the file (or the `.xmp`) back exactly as it was. **Several at once:** select photos in the grid, then Info → **Edit N photos**. A field that differs between them says *Mixed* and is left as each has it unless you change it; a field you change is set on all of them. A keyword only some have shows how many (*Christmas · 2 of 4*): click it to give it to all, × takes it off all. One `Ctrl+Z` puts them all back.
  - **Date tools, for scans:** **⋯** beside Taken. **Set one date for all…**; **Shift by an amount…** (years, days, hours and minutes, back or on, for a camera whose clock was wrong; a photo with no date is left as it is); **Number in grid order…** (from a start, each one some seconds, minutes, hours or days after the one before, in the order the grid shows them, so a scanned order sorts as it was scanned). Each shows the new dates before it sets them, and one `Ctrl+Z` takes them all back.
  - **All fields…** (one photo): every EXIF, IPTC and XMP field the photo has, with **Find a field**. Change a value, **−** removes a field, and **Add field** puts in an XMP one (*prefix:Name*, as *photoshop:City*; the common ones are suggested) or an EXIF one (picked from the list, as *Lens model*). Fields jEditor keeps right itself (the orientation, the size, the camera's maker note) are gray. For a RAW, its own EXIF is shown but not changed, and everything goes in its `.xmp`. **Save** writes it in, with `Ctrl+Z` to undo.
  - **Presets:** in the form, **Presets → Save these as a preset…** keeps the author, the copyright and the keywords (those all the photos will have) under a name; picking it from **Presets** later fills them in, on one photo or a selection, and **Done** saves them. **Delete a preset** is there too.
- **Folder details.** Press `I` in the grid, or right-click a folder for **Folder info** (in a theme, the bottom of the folder list shows the total for what you're looking at, `1,240 photos · 4.80 GB`, and a click on it opens it too). It lists how many photos there are (directly in the folder and with its subfolders), the total, average and largest size, file types, dates and flags. For the whole order it also shows how much room `.jeditor` takes: trash, cache and Auto crop originals. Select several photos to see the same for the selection.

`Ctrl+Z` undoes a move or copy in one step, together with any photos it replaced and the folder made for it. Making, renaming and deleting folders can be undone too.

## Renaming

Press `F2`, use **Rename…** on the selection bar, or right-click the grid background for **Rename all…**. It works on the selected photos or the whole folder.

- **New names** builds names from a pattern, such as `Smith_{###}` → `Smith_001.jpg`, `Smith_002.jpg`, …
- **Find & replace** changes part of the existing names, such as replacing `IMG_` with `Smith_`.

Patterns can use `{###}` for a sequence number (one digit per `#`), `{name}` for the current name, and `{date}` / `{time}` for when the photo was taken. Numbers follow the order you choose: the current sort, date taken, name, date modified or file size, ascending or descending, from any start number and step.

The preview shows every old and new name before anything changes. Duplicate names, names already used by other files, and characters Windows doesn't allow are flagged, and the rename won't run until they're fixed. The file extension is always kept, and `Ctrl+Z` undoes the whole rename in one step.

## Finding duplicates (work in progress)

Press `D` in a folder. It looks for:

- exact copies (identical file contents), and
- the same print scanned more than once, including when it was turned, placed at a different angle, cropped slightly differently, exposed differently, or scanned in black and white.

Scanner bed and film borders are ignored when comparing. Every group is shown on one page that scrolls, a row of thumbnails each, with the size and date under each photo and how alike it is to the one kept. In each group the copy with the most pixels is kept and the others are marked **Trash**; click a photo to keep it or mark it. **Not duplicates** leaves a group alone. **Trash N photos** (`Enter`) moves every marked photo to the trash at once, and one `Ctrl+Z` brings them back. `K` goes back to the suggested keepers.

**Show in grid** (`G`) shows the duplicates in the grid instead: each group on its own rows under a heading, the suggested keeper first with a **Keep** badge, and the marked photos already selected, so the selection bar's Delete trashes them. Everything else in the grid works as usual (open, rotate, flag, move). A **Duplicates (N)** chip beside the search says it's on; click it to see every photo again. When no group has two photos left, the grid shows everything again by itself.

It can still miss some re-scans or group photos that look alike, so check each group before trashing. The **Sensitivity** setting trades one for the other.

## If it gets slow

Turn on **Debug console** in Settings → General → Troubleshooting (it's off to start with), then press `Ctrl+Shift+D` to open it. The yellow lines at the top show what the app is holding and doing: memory, undo history, images decoded, background work, and how long recent photos took to load. A screenshot of it taken while things are slow is the most useful thing to include in a bug report.

It's built and tested for orders of 500–2000 photos. With 2000 scans at 1800×1200: the folder opens and shows the first photo in under half a second, thumbnails take about 20 seconds in the background the first time (under 2 seconds when it's opened again, rotated or not), rotating all 2000 takes about 8 seconds, renaming them about 2 seconds, and a duplicate scan about 20 seconds. Bigger files take longer to thumbnail, though big JPEGs are decoded at a fraction of their size for thumbnails and previews (about 4× faster than a full decode on 20-megapixel scans).

Undo keeps up to 50 steps. Rotating a selection in the grid is one step, however many photos it covers. Rotations are undone by rotating back, so they don't use memory. Crops keep a copy of the original file so they can be undone, up to 256 MB in total; older crop steps are dropped after that.

## Where things are in the code

The UI is plain JavaScript with no dependencies, in `app/`:

- `script.js`: viewer, grid, rotation, file operations, shortcuts
- `crop.js`: crop and straighten
- `develop.js`, `adjust.js`, `adjust-masks.js`, `adjust-spots.js`: Adjust (the maths, the panel, masks, spot removal)
- `retouch.js`: spot and dust removal, perspective (with auto perspective) and lens fixes, done on the photo before it's cropped
- `select.js`: finding the sky and the subject (masks)
- `raw.js`: RAW files, from the JPEG inside them
- `paint.js`, `paint-editor.js`, `paint-heal.js`: Paint (the layers kept with the photo, the screen, the healing, tone and focus tools' pixels, healed the way `retouch.js` does)
- `import.js`: importing from a camera's card
- `blank.js`: Paint's start: a new canvas (right-click a folder, or the start page's Paint), or an image or folder to open
- `themes.js`, `themes/`: the themes (Settings → Appearance): the list, their stylesheets, the title bar and menu bar they draw, 1984's dithered thumbnails
- `modes.js`: Photos, Adjust and Paint as modes: the switch (sidebar, chip), the strip in Adjust and Paint
- `disc.js`: burning a CD or DVD (the Windows app's `src/Burn.cs`)
- `export.js`: the Export window (a USB drive, a disc, or copies)
- `convert.js`: Convert (photos to AVIF, JPEG XL or WebP: the quality and close-up, the storage now and after, the dots)
- `codec.js`, `codec-worker.js`, `codecs/`, `tiff-codec.js`: writing AVIF, JPEG XL, WebP, JPEG, PNG and TIFF, and reading JPEG XL, HEIC and TIFF, in workers (libavif, libjxl and libheif as WebAssembly; TIFF jEditor's own)
- `crop-auto.js`: finding the print on a scan (Auto)
- `crop-boxes.js`: Auto's map-to-boxes finder and full-detail edge fit (being scored; `CropAuto.find(..., { boxes: true })`)
- `changelog.js`: the list of changes shown in What's new, and the release notes
- `meta.js`: reading and copying JPEG/PNG metadata, and EXIF in WebP, AVIF and JPEG XL
- `meta-fields.js`: the fields Info's Edit changes (title, caption, keywords, rating, author, copyright, date taken, place): read from XMP, IPTC and EXIF, and written into JPEG, PNG, WebP and TIFF without re-encoding, or into an `.xmp` beside a RAW
- `meta-editor.js`: Info's Edit: the form, a selection's Mixed fields, the date tools, All fields, presets, saving with one undo step
- `dupes.js`: duplicate finder
- `rename.js`: batch rename
- `folders.js`: folders: the folder list, moving and copying photos, making, renaming and deleting folders
- `flags.js`: flags (star, rescan, color labels) and search
- `watch.js`: watching the folder for changes, and rescanning
- `start.js`: Home: its tiles, the Settings menu (When jEditor opens), the update dot, All projects, and on the web the app for this computer
- `palette.js`: Find anything (`Ctrl+K`): the box, what it finds where you are (the shortcuts' commands, the theme menus' own, photos, folders, projects), matching and ranking, and running the one picked
- `places.js`: the sidebar's places in the apps: the project's name and its picker (find a project), Recents, pinned folders and drives with the room on each, renaming a project in place (and its Undo), and All projects… on Home

- `ui.js`: the app's own alert / confirm / prompt dialogs, and focus handling for every dialog

## Where projects and the cache are kept

Settings → General → Folders (the Windows, Mac and Linux apps):

- **Projects folder**: choose the folder that holds your photo folders. Each folder inside it shows as a project. Change it later and jEditor asks whether to move the projects there or leave them, saying how many and how big. It checks the free space first: if there isn't enough, it says so with the numbers and moves nothing. On the same drive each project is moved whole, instantly. To another drive, each one is copied, then checked file by file against the original, and only then is the original removed. If anything fails, the move stops there: what's moved is in the new folder, the rest are where they were, and nothing is lost. A project whose name is already taken in the new folder is left where it is. A whole drive or your user folder is never moved, only left.
- **Cache folder** (the Windows app): where jEditor keeps each project's thumbnails, flags and edits. Changing it moves them the same way, into a **jEditor cache** folder inside the one you choose.
- **Cache** and **Clear cache…**: how much room the thumbnails take, and clearing them. Projects stay; on Home they're ghost stacks with their names until their thumbnails are made again. Your photos, flags and edits aren't touched.
